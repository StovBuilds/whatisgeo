Surfer sells two different kinds of AI product under one brand, so each needs its own verdict. The writing and scoring tools help you produce and tune pages. The AI Tracker reports how often AI assistants mention you. Neither can guarantee rankings or citations, and Google's own documentation explains why.

Throughout this page we keep three kinds of claim apart: what Surfer says about its own product, what Google documents, and what independent research found. Prices and plan details come from Surfer's pricing page and help centre, read on 25 October 2026.

## Is Surfer SEO an AI tool?

Yes. Surfer's pricing page pitches it to "teams that want to win AI search", and every plan it lists includes AI features. In Surfer's own words, these include:

- **Content Editor and Content Score:** you write or paste a draft for a target keyword and get live guidelines and a score from 0 to 100.
- **AI writing (Surfy):** an AI writing assistant inside the editor. Surfer also lists "1-click content optimization".
- **AI Detector and Humanizer:** a classifier that estimates whether text is machine-written, and a rewriter. We cover both on separate pages: the [Surfer AI content detector](/surfer-seo-ai-content-detector/) and the [free AI humanizer](/surfer-seo-free-ai-humanizer/).
- **AI Tracker:** monitoring of brand mentions in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Google AI Overviews and Gemini. It reports a Visibility Score, mention gaps, share of voice and sentiment.

Calling it "an AI tool" is accurate but not very useful. What matters is which of these you would use and what it is supposed to change.

## The two things "Surfer AI" can mean

Search the phrase and you land on two products sharing one name.

The first is the content side: the editor, the score and the AI writing assistant. It helps you write, and its output is a page.

The second is the AI Tracker. It does not write anything. It runs a set of prompts through AI assistants and reports whether your brand appears. Surfer also sells it on its own as a plan called AI Search Analytics.

It is easy to blur the two. Praise for the editor tells you nothing about the tracker, and the reverse is also true. Judge each against the job you need done.

## What the AI writing and content features do, and what Google says about AI content

**How Surfer says the score works.** According to Surfer's help centre, Content Score has two parts. The SEO Score looks at "keyword usage, topical coverage, structure, and alignment with top-performing pages for your target query". It gives most weight to terms that "appear consistently across top-performing pages". The AI Search Score is based on "Facts Coverage" and "Upfront Intent Alignment": whether you cover the facts AI answers tend to include, and whether you answer the main question early. Surfer notes that the AI Search Score does not yet take competitors into account. The FAQ on its pricing page also says the guidelines draw on "over 500 web and AI signals". That figure is Surfer's own, and Surfer does not publish the list.

Two details from the same help article are worth knowing before a trial. First, Surfer advises aiming 10 to 20 points above competitors instead of chasing 100, and says over-optimising "can actually hurt your final content". Second, the score needs competitor pages from at least three different domains, so branded keywords and other narrow searches can return a score of 0.

**What that means in practice.** A score built from top-ranking pages tells you how closely your draft resembles what already ranks. That makes a reasonable coverage checklist. It cannot tell you whether your page is accurate, original or more useful than the others, and that is what Google says it rewards.

**What Google says.** Google's 2023 guidance is titled "Rewarding high-quality content, however it is produced" and says "appropriate use of AI or automation is not against our guidelines". Its current page on generative AI content adds that it is "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing". Its spam policies define scaled content abuse as generating many pages "for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users". One of the examples they list is "using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users".

So by Google's documented rules, it is fine to use Surfy to draft a page that you then check, correct and improve with your own knowledge. Using any AI writer to turn out large numbers of thin pages is the pattern Google names as spam. The tool does not decide which side you are on; your process does. For the wider question, see [is AI content good for SEO](/is-ai-content-good-for-seo/).

## What the AI visibility tracker does, and how to read its numbers

Surfer says the AI Tracker shows "how Gemini, ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, and Perplexity talk about your brand". You add the prompts you care about. The tool then reports mentions, a Visibility Score, share of voice against competitors, sentiment, and the sources the AI answers drew on.

Read its numbers with care. The points below are our advice; they are not from Surfer's documentation:

- **It measures a sample.** A mention rate covers only the prompts in your project. Choose different prompts and the number changes.
- **AI answers vary.** The same question can get different answers from one run to the next, so one day's figure is a snapshot.
- **Coverage depends on the plan.** On the pricing page we read, the Standard plan card lists 25 prompts in ChatGPT, refreshed weekly. Pro lists 50 prompts refreshed daily across all five engines.
- **Tools disagree.** Two trackers with different prompts and schedules can report different scores for the same brand without either being broken.

Before you trust a figure, ask:

- Which prompts are tracked, and who chose them?
- How often do they run, and on which engines?
- Does the report show the actual answers and citations, or only a headline score?

Our guide to [measuring AI visibility](/how-to-measure-ai-visibility/) goes through this in more detail.

**Can content changes move those numbers?** There is some research. Aggarwal et al. (GEO, KDD 2024) built a benchmark of 10,000 queries called GEO-bench. They tested nine ways of rewriting source pages in a generative engine they set up themselves, which fetched the top five Google results for each query. Adding citations, quotations and statistics did best, raising the paper's own visibility measures by up to 40%. Keyword stuffing did poorly. They also ran tests on Perplexity. The results varied by subject area, and they came from a research setup, so they may not carry over to today's commercial assistants. The paper does not test Surfer or any other commercial tool.

Google's position on its own AI features is simpler: "specific optimization isn't required for AI Overviews and AI Mode". A page must be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet, and "there are no additional technical requirements". Normal SEO practice applies.

## Can you use Surfer for free, and what does it cost?

**Free:** We found no free plan on Surfer's pricing page on 25 October 2026. Surfer does offer a 7-day trial with Pro-level access. Its help centre explains the terms:

- You choose a plan and enter card details at checkout.
- The trial "does not cancel automatically". If you don't cancel within 7 days, you are charged for the plan you chose.

## How to test it yourself before you pay

Seven days goes quickly, so plan the test before you start the trial. These steps are our recommendation:

1. **Set a reminder to cancel** on day six, because the trial turns into a paid plan automatically.
2. **Pick one real page** you already want to improve, on a topic you know well.
3. **Run it through the Content Editor.** Note every suggestion, accept the ones you agree with and reject the rest. Write down which was which and why.
4. **Fact-check anything Surfy writes** before it goes on the page, as Google's guidance asks.
5. **Time the work** and compare it with how long the same edit would take without the tool.
6. **If you care about the tracker,** list ten questions a real customer would ask and add them as prompts. Then type a few into ChatGPT and Perplexity yourself, and compare what you see with what the tracker reports.
7. **Decide on what you found.** If the suggestions held up and the work went faster, that is a reason to pay. If you cannot tell, that is an answer too.

You will not see ranking changes inside a week. Judge the trial on whether the suggestions were useful and accurate.

**Is it legit?** Surfer is a real company with published plans, a help centre and documented cancellation terms. It says it has more than 150,000 customers, which is its own figure. Review sites carry plenty of user opinions. Give more weight to reviewers who say what they used it for and for how long. Be cautious of reviews that repeat Surfer's marketing wording or send you to it through affiliate links. A star rating tells you whether people were satisfied, not whether the tool fits your work.

## Who it suits, and who can skip it

The content tools suit people who:

- publish regularly on topics they know
- want a structured check of their coverage against what already ranks
- will edit and fact-check AI drafts themselves

The tracker suits teams who want a regular view of brand mentions in AI answers and understand that it reports on a chosen sample of prompts.

You can probably skip it if you publish rarely, if your content lacks something people actually want, or if you expect the tool to supply expertise. A score cannot fix those. If you only need tracking, the standalone plan or a different tracker may fit better. Our overview of [AI SEO tools](/ai-seo-tools/) sets out the options without ranking them.

Whatever you choose, the method is the same. Decide what you are trying to change, test on a real page, and judge by what you measured. Our home guide covers [how to measure progress](/#measurement).
